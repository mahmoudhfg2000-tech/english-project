Part 1 · Mahmoud Hisham

Who are you online?

Identification

Say who you are

A username or an email. Anyone can type your name, so this alone proves nothing.

Authentication

Prove you really are you

A password, a fingerprint or a code. This is what separates you from someone pretending to be you.

Three ways to prove it

Something you know

A password, a PIN, a secret answer.

Something you have

Your phone, an SMS code, a security key.

Something you are

Your fingerprint or your face.

Any two together = two-factor authentication (2FA)

Even if someone learns your password, they still need your phone. Turn it on for your email and social media today.

Part 1 · Wrap-up

Five golden rules

Long sentence

At least 12 characters. String together a few random words you can remember.

One per site

If one site leaks, your other accounts stay safe.

Turn on 2FA

A code on your phone stops most break-in attempts.

Password manager

An app that creates and remembers passwords so you don't have to.

Think before you click

A link that rushes you to log in? Be suspicious. Go to the site yourself.

Cyber­bullying

Presented byTayem Elbendary
Part 2 · Tayem Elbendary

Bullying online is not a joke

Hurting someone on purpose through messages, photos or comments online. It usually repeats, and lots of people can see it.

What it looks like

Threats and insults

In private messages or group chats.

Sharing photos or secrets

Without the owner's permission.

Impersonation

A fake account in someone's name posting nasty things.

Exclusion

Kicking someone out of a group or game on purpose.

Mocking and cruel comments

"I was just joking" doesn't undo the hurt.

Abuse in online games

Still bullying.

Why is it harder than bullying in person?

It follows you home. It stays recorded. The audience is huge. And sometimes the bully hides behind an anonymous account.

Scams

Presented byMahmoud Hisham
Part 4 · Scams · Mahmoud Hisham

Scammers don't hack computers first. They hack feelings.

Excitement, fear and trust. Almost every scam runs the same four steps.

1

Bait

A free prize, a scary warning, a "friend" in trouble. Anything that grabs your attention.

2

Trust

A copied logo, a stolen profile photo, a name you recognise.

3

Pressure

"Right now! Only 10 minutes left! Don't tell anyone!"

4

Payoff

Your money, a code, a password, or private photos.

Break the chain and the scam fails.

The easiest link to break is step 3. When something is urgent and secret, slow down on purpose.

Part 4 · Where they hide

Scams show up where we hang out

A familiar logo, username or profile picture does not prove a message is real.

Gaming

Free skins or currency

A link promises game rewards, then asks you to sign in or share a code.

Social apps

Giveaway or creator DM

"You won!" But first pay a fee, click a link, or send personal details.

Buying & selling

Fake buyer or seller

They push you off the platform, invent a payment problem, or ask for a verification code.

Study & work

Easy-money offer

A "job" asks for an upfront fee, bank details or your ID before explaining the work.

Pause when you see pressure, secrecy, unexpected prizes, or a request for money or codes.

Scammers can copy names and images. Verify with the person or organization through a separate, official channel.

    Real link or fake?

    The trick

    The real owner is the last words before the first single "/". Read that part from right to left.

    The padlock myth

    A padlock only means the connection is encrypted. Scam sites have padlocks too.

    Part 4 · Know the script

    Scripts you will actually hear

    Fake support

    Your wallet is suspended. Send us the code to reactivate it.

    Your move: Support never needs your PIN or code. Hang up and open the official app yourself.

    Mistaken transfer

    I sent you money by mistake. Please send it back.

    Your move: Check your balance in the official app. Let the bank handle any reversal.

    Parcel fee

    Your parcel is on hold. Pay a small fee here.

    Your move: Track it in the courier's own app or site, never through the message link.

    Friend in trouble

    It's me, new number. Can you lend me money fast?

    Your move: Call your friend on the old number or send a voice note before doing anything.

    Easy job

    Earn big money from your phone. Just pay a registration fee.

    Your move: Real employers don't charge you to hire you.

    Quick investment

    Put in a little today and get triple tomorrow. Guaranteed.

    Your move: Guaranteed fast profit is the oldest scam line. Walk away.

    Part 4 · The serious one

    When a scam turns into blackmail

    Someone you only know online gets private photos or secrets from you, then threatens to share them unless you pay or send more. It is a crime, and being tricked is never your fault.

    1

    Don't pay, don't send more

    Paying rarely ends it. They come back for more.

    2

    Keep the evidence

    Screenshot the chat, username, links and dates. Don't delete anything yet.

    3

    Block and report

    Report the account in the app, then cut contact.

    4

    Tell an adult you trust

    A parent, a teacher, a counselor. You don't have to carry it alone.

    Prevent it

    Never send private photos to someone you only know online. A reverse image search can show whether a profile photo is stolen.

    Help removing images

    Take It Down (under 18) and StopNCII.org (18+) help stop intimate images spreading. In Egypt, the child helpline is 16000.

    Part 4 · Your response

    Stop the scam. Keep control.

    1

    Stop

    Do not click, pay, reply or share a code. Do not keep secrets for the sender.

    2

    Verify elsewhere

    Open the official app yourself, or contact the person using a number you already trust.

    3

    Tell someone

    Show a trusted adult. Save the message and account details before reporting and blocking.

    4

    If you already clicked

    Tell an adult right away. From the official site, change the affected password, secure your email, and contact your bank or payment provider if money or card details were shared.

    Important: A real support agent should never ask you to send them a one-time login code or password.
